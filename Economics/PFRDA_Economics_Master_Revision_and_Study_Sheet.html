Chapter 01: Introduction to Demand & Supply

Demand & Supply, PPC, Shifts vs. Movements, Market Equilibrium

📊 Visual Diagram 1.1: Production Possibility Curve (PPC), Demand-Supply Equilibrium & Shift vs. Movement
📐 Key Mathematical Formulas, Equations & Equilibrium Conditions
  • Linear Demand Function: Qd = a − bP (where 'a' = autonomous demand intercept, '−b' = ΔQd/ΔP = negative slope)
  • Linear Supply Function: Qs = c + dP (where 'c' = supply intercept, '+d' = ΔQs/ΔP = positive slope)
  • Market Equilibrium Condition: Qd = Qs ⇒ a − bP* = c + dP* ⇒ Equilibrium Price (P*) = (a − c) ÷ (b + d)
  • Marginal Opportunity Cost (MOC / MRT along PPC): ΔY Sacrificed ÷ ΔX Gained (Rising MOC makes PPC concave to origin)

🧮 Exam-Style Worked Numerical / Analytical Application:
Worked Numerical: Given Market Demand Qd = 100 − 4P and Market Supply Qs = −20 + 2P. At equilibrium, Qd = Qs ⇒ 100 − 4P = −20 + 2P ⇒ 6P = 120 ⇒ Equilibrium Price (P*) = ₹20. Substituting P* = 20 gives Equilibrium Quantity (Q*) = 100 − 4(20) = 20 units. If government imposes a price ceiling at P = ₹15, Qd = 40 and Qs = 10, creating an Excess Demand (Shortage) of 30 units.
🎯 High-Yield Conceptual Pillars & PYQ Exam Traps
  • Central Problem of Scarcity & Choice: Human wants are unlimited and recurring, whereas resources (Land, Labour, Capital, Entrepreneurship) are scarce and have alternative uses. Opportunity Cost is the value of the next best alternative foregone.
  • Production Possibility Curve (PPC / Transformation Curve): Shows maximum attainable combinations of two goods with fixed resources and technology. Points inside PPC = unemployment/inefficiency; points on PPC = full & efficient utilization; points outside PPC = unattainable without resource/tech growth. PPC is concave to the origin when Marginal Opportunity Cost (MOC) is increasing, linear when MOC is constant, and convex when MOC is decreasing.
  • Effective Demand (3 Pillars): Desire for a commodity + Ability to pay (purchasing power) + Willingness to spend at a given price and time.
  • Why the Demand Curve Slopes Downward: (1) Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility (Marshall — consumer equates MU = P), (2) Substitution Effect (Hicks/Slutsky — cheaper good replaces relatively costlier substitutes), (3) Income Effect (fall in price raises real purchasing power P_real), (4) Arrival of new consumers at lower prices, and (5) Multiple uses of a commodity (e.g., electricity/milk).
  • Simultaneous Shifts in Demand & Supply: (1) D↑ and S↑ ⇒ Equilibrium Q* unambiguously rises, while P* is indeterminate (rises if ΔD > ΔS, falls if ΔS > ΔD, constant if ΔD = ΔS); (2) D↑ and S↓ ⇒ Equilibrium P* unambiguously rises, while Q* is indeterminate; (3) D↓ and S↑ ⇒ Equilibrium P* unambiguously falls, while Q* is indeterminate.

Chapter 02: Elasticity of Demand & Supply

Price, Income & Cross Elasticity, Point/Arc Methods, Total Outlay & Slope

📊 Visual Diagram 2.1: 5 Degrees of Price Elasticity, Point Elasticity on Linear Demand Curve & Total Outlay Matrix
📐 Key Mathematical Formulas, Equations & Equilibrium Conditions
  • Point Price Elasticity of Demand (Ep): (−dQ / dP) × (P / Q) = Lower Segment of Linear Demand Curve ÷ Upper Segment
  • Arc / Midpoint Price Elasticity: [(Q2 − Q1) ÷ ((Q1 + Q2)/2)] ÷ [(P2 − P1) ÷ ((P1 + P2)/2)] = [(ΔQ / ΔP) × ((P1 + P2) / (Q1 + Q2))]
  • Relationship between AR, MR & Elasticity: MR = AR × [1 − (1 / |Ep|)] (At |Ep| = 1, MR = 0 and Total Revenue is Maximum)
  • Cross-Price Elasticity (Ec): (% ΔQx) ÷ (% ΔPy) = (ΔQx / ΔPy) × (Py / Qx) (+ve = Substitutes; −ve = Complements; 0 = Unrelated)
  • Income Elasticity (Ey): (% ΔQ) ÷ (% ΔY) = (ΔQ / ΔY) × (Y / Q) (Ey > 1 Luxury; 0 < Ey < 1 Necessity; Ey < 0 Inferior Good)

🧮 Exam-Style Worked Numerical / Analytical Application:
Worked Numerical (PYQ Pattern): When the price of a commodity falls from ₹12 to ₹8, quantity demanded rises from 100 units to 150 units. (a) Standard Point Elasticity = (50 / −4) × (12 / 100) = −1.5 (|Ep| = 1.5). (b) Arc/Midpoint Elasticity = (50 / −4) × ((12 + 8) / (100 + 150)) = (50 / −4) × (20 / 250) = −1.0 (Unitary Elastic, note TR1 = 12×100 = ₹1,200 and TR2 = 8×150 = ₹1,200!).
🎯 High-Yield Conceptual Pillars & PYQ Exam Traps
  • Slope vs. Elasticity: Slope of demand curve is ΔP/ΔQ, whereas Price Elasticity is (ΔQ/ΔP)×(P/Q) = (1/Slope)×(P/Q). Along a straight-line downward-sloping demand curve, the slope is constant everywhere, but elasticity varies from ∞ at the price axis to 1 at the midpoint to 0 at the quantity axis.
  • Two Intersecting Linear Demand Curves: At the exact point of intersection (same P and Q), the flatter demand curve is more elastic than the steeper demand curve because its inverse slope (dQ/dP) is larger.
  • Determinants of Price Elasticity: Availability of close substitutes (more substitutes ⇒ higher elasticity), proportion of income spent on the good (larger share ⇒ more elastic), nature of commodity (necessities inelastic; luxuries elastic), number of uses (more uses ⇒ more elastic), and time horizon (long-run demand is more elastic than short-run demand).
  • Engel's Law & Income Elasticity: Ernst Engel showed that as household income rises, the percentage of income spent on food declines (0 < Ey < 1 for food necessities), while the share spent on luxuries/services rises (Ey > 1).
  • Elasticity of Supply (Es): Any straight-line supply curve passing through the origin has Es = 1 everywhere regardless of its angle; if it intersects the positive Y-axis (price axis), Es > 1; if it intersects the positive X-axis (quantity axis), Es < 1.

Chapter 03: Theory of Production, Revenue & Cost

Law of Variable Proportions, Returns to Scale, Isoquants, Short/Long-Run Cost & Revenue Curves

📐 Key Mathematical Formulas, Equations & Equilibrium Conditions
  • Average Product (AP): TP ÷ L | Marginal Product (MP): ΔTP ÷ ΔL = d(TP)/dL (When AP is maximum, MP = AP)
  • Cobb-Douglas Production Function: Q = A · L^α · K^β (α + β = 1 Constant Returns; α + β > 1 Increasing Returns; α + β < 1 Decreasing Returns)
  • Least-Cost Producer Equilibrium (Tangency of Isoquant & Isocost): MRTS_LK = MPL ÷ MPK = Wage (w) ÷ Rental Rate of Capital (r)
  • Short-Run Cost Identities: TC = TFC + TVC | AC = AFC + AVC | MC = ΔTC/ΔQ = ΔTVC/ΔQ
  • Revenue Identities: TR = P × Q | AR = TR ÷ Q = P | MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ = d(TR)/dQ

🧮 Exam-Style Worked Numerical / Analytical Application:
Worked Numerical: A firm's short-run total cost function is TC = 200 + 10Q − 2Q² + (1/3)Q³. Here TFC = 200; TVC = 10Q − 2Q² + (1/3)Q³; AVC = 10 − 2Q + (1/3)Q²; and Marginal Cost MC = d(TC)/dQ = 10 − 4Q + Q². 🎯 High-Yield Conceptual Pillars & PYQ Exam Traps
  • Short Run vs. Long Run: In the short run, at least one factor (capital/plant) is fixed and output varies via the Law of Variable Proportions. In the long run, all factors are variable and scale changes via Returns to Scale (Increasing, Constant, Decreasing).
  • Isoquants (Equal-Product Curves): Downward sloping, convex to origin due to Diminishing Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution (MRTS_LK = −ΔK/ΔL = MPL/MPK), and never intersect. Special shapes: Linear Isoquant (perfectly substitutable inputs, constant MRTS); Right-Angle L-Shaped Leontief Isoquant (perfectly complementary inputs used in fixed proportions, zero substitutability).
  • Shape of Short-Run Cost Curves: (1) AFC (TFC/Q) is a Rectangular Hyperbola that falls continuously as output expands and never touches either axis; (2) AVC, AC (ATC), and MC are U-shaped due to the Law of Variable Proportions; (3) MC intersects both AVC and AC at their respective minimum points; (4) Minimum point of AVC occurs at a lower output level than the minimum point of AC because falling AFC pulls AC down even after AVC starts rising.
  • Long-Run Average Cost (LAC / Envelope Curve / Planning Curve): Envelopes a family of Short-Run Average Cost (SAC) curves. In the falling phase of LAC (Economies of Scale), LAC is tangent to the left of the minimum points of SACs; at the minimum point of LAC (Optimum Plant Size), LAC = LMC = SAC = SMC; in the rising phase (Diseconomies of Scale), LAC is tangent to the right of SAC minima.
  • Internal vs. External Economies of Scale: Internal economies (technical, managerial, commercial, financial, risk-bearing) accrue to an individual firm as it expands its own scale (moving along the LAC curve). External economies accrue to all firms in an industry as the entire industry/cluster grows (shifting the entire cost curves downward).

Chapter 04: Market Structure I: Perfect Competition & Monopoly

Price Taking, Shut-Down Point, Monopoly Barriers, Lerner Index & Price Discrimination (1st, 2nd, 3rd Degree)

📐 Key Mathematical Formulas, Equations & Equilibrium Conditions
  • Profit-Maximizing Equilibrium (All Markets): (1) First-Order Condition: MR = MC; (2) Second-Order Condition: MC must cut MR from below (Slope of MC > Slope of MR)
  • Perfect Competition Firm Demand: P = AR = MR (Horizontal line at market price P*)
  • Short-Run Shut-Down Rule (Perfect Competition): Operate if P ≥ Minimum AVC; Shut down if P < Minimum AVC (Loss = TFC)
  • Lerner Index of Monopoly Power (Abba Lerner): L = (P − MC) ÷ P = 1 ÷ |Ep| (0 for Perfect Competition; between 0 and 1 for Monopoly)
  • Third-Degree Price Discrimination Equilibrium: MR1 = MR2 = MC ⇒ P1[1 − (1/|e1|)] = P2[1 − (1/|e2|)] (Higher price charged in the less elastic market!)

🧮 Exam-Style Worked Numerical / Analytical Application:
Worked Numerical: A monopolist faces inverse demand P = 100 − 2Q and has constant marginal cost MC = ₹20 (with zero fixed cost). Total Revenue TR = P·Q = 100Q − 2Q² ⇒ Marginal Revenue MR = 100 − 4Q. Setting MR = MC ⇒ 100 − 4Q = 20 ⇒ Q_monopoly = 20 units, and P_monopoly = 100 − 2(20) = ₹60 (Profit = (60−20)×20 = ₹800). Under Perfect Competition (P = MC = 20), Q_pc = 40 units. Deadweight Loss of Monopoly = ½ × (P_m − MC) × (Q_pc − Q_m) = ½ × (60 − 20) × (40 − 20) = ₹400.
🎯 High-Yield Conceptual Pillars & PYQ Exam Traps
  • Monopoly Never Operates in the Inelastic Portion of Demand (|Ep| < 1): Since MR = P[1 − 1/|Ep|] and Marginal Cost (MC) is always non-negative (MC ≥ 0), profit maximization (MR = MC ≥ 0) requires |Ep| ≥ 1. If |Ep| < 1, MR is negative, so the monopolist will always raise price and cut output until reaching the elastic zone (|Ep| ≥ 1).
  • Natural Monopoly: Arises when massive fixed infrastructure costs create continuous Economies of Scale (sub-additive costs / declining LAC) over the entire market demand range (e.g., power transmission grids, railways, municipal water pipelines), making a single provider more cost-efficient than multiple competing firms.

Chapter 05: Market Structure II: Monopolistic Competition & Oligopoly

Product Differentiation, Excess Capacity, Cournot, Bertrand, Edgeworth, Chamberlin, Sweezy Kinked Demand, Stackelberg, Cartels & Price Leadership

📊 Visual Diagram 5.1: Market Structures Master Comparison & Oligopoly Models (Cournot, Bertrand, Sweezy, Stackelberg)
📐 Key Mathematical Formulas, Equations & Equilibrium Conditions
  • Excess Capacity in Monopolistic Competition (Long Run): Q_optimal (at Minimum LAC) − Q_actual (at tangency where P = LAC > LMC = MR)
  • Cournot Duopoly Reaction Functions & Equilibrium (Zero MC, Linear D): Each duopolist produces 1/3 of Competitive Output (Total Q = 2/3 Q_pc; generalize for N firms: Q_total = [N/(N+1)]·Q_pc)
  • Bertrand Duopoly Equilibrium (Homogeneous Product, Price Competition): P = MC (Identical to Perfect Competition equilibrium even with just 2 firms!)
  • Stackelberg Duopoly (Sequential Quantity Leadership): Leader produces 1/2 of Q_pc; Follower produces 1/4 of Q_pc (Total Q = 3/4 Q_pc)

🎯 High-Yield Conceptual Pillars & PYQ Exam Traps
  • Production Costs vs. Selling Costs (Chamberlin): Production costs are incurred to create/manufacture a product to satisfy a given demand, whereas Selling Costs (advertising, sales promotion) are incurred specifically to shift the demand curve to the right and make it less price-elastic by building brand loyalty.
  • Group Equilibrium (Chamberlin): Because products in monopolistic competition are differentiated rather than identical, Chamberlin replaced Marshall's 'Industry' with a 'Group' of firms producing closely related differentiated products (using dd for proportional group price changes and DD for single-firm price changes).

  • Paradox of Thrift (Keynes): If all households simultaneously attempt to save a larger fraction of their income (autonomous saving ↑ / MPS ↑), aggregate consumption and effective demand fall, causing equilibrium national income (Y) to contract via the multiplier until total realized saving (S = I) is either unchanged (if investment is autonomous) or actually lower (if investment is induced by income)!
