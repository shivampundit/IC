1.1 Institutional Governance for Financial Stability — FSDC, BFS & BPSS

  • Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC, established Dec 2010 on Raghuram Rajan Committee recommendation): Non-statutory apex inter-regulatory body chaired by the Union Finance Minister (members include RBI Governor, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, IBBI, and IFSCA Chairpersons, plus Finance Ministry Secretaries). The FSDC Sub-Committee is chaired by the RBI Governor.
  • Board for Financial Supervision (BFS, 1994): Committee of the RBI Central Board (chaired by the RBI Governor with 4 Deputy Governors as ex-officio members and 4 Central Board directors) that oversees integrated supervision of banks, AIFIs, and NBFCs.
  • Board for Regulation and Supervision of Payment and Settlement Systems (BPSS, 2005): Established under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (chaired by RBI Governor) to regulate payment systems, NPCI, card networks, and cross-border payment aggregators.

2.1 Universal Banking On-Tap Licensing Norms (2016–2026 Amendments)

  • Minimum Paid-Up Voting Equity Capital for a New Universal Bank: Raised to ₹1,000 Crore (up from ₹500 Crore earlier). Promoters must hold a minimum of 40% of paid-up voting equity capital locked in for 5 years, gradually brought down to 26% within 15 years. Foreign shareholding is capped at 74% (up to 49% under Automatic Route, and >49% up to 74% under Government Route). At least 25% of branches must be opened in unbanked rural centres (population < 9,999).

5.1 Primary vs. Secondary Market Auction Mechanisms (Multiple Price vs. Uniform Price Auctions)

  • Multiple Price (French) Auction: Successful competitive bidders pay the exact price (or yield) they individually bid at the auction.
  • Uniform Price (Dutch) Auction: All successful competitive and non-competitive bidders pay the same single cut-off price (or receive the cut-off yield) determined at the auction. Under RBI's Retail Direct and Non-Competitive Bidding (NCB) facility, 5% of notified G-Sec/T-Bill/SDL auction amounts is reserved for retail investors at the weighted average/cut-off price.

8.1 Six Key Risks Faced by Bond Investors

  • 1. Interest Rate (Price) Risk: Risk that rising market interest rates reduce the market price of existing fixed-coupon bonds.
  • 2. Reinvestment Risk: Risk that falling market interest rates force the investor to reinvest periodic coupon cash flows at a lower rate than the initial YTM (Note: Price Risk and Reinvestment Risk move in opposite directions and exactly offset each other when the investor's holding horizon equals the bond's Macaulay Duration — known as Portfolio Immunization!).
  • 3. Credit / Default Risk & Downgrade Risk: Risk that the issuer fails to pay timely coupon/principal (measured by credit spread over sovereign G-Secs).
  • 4. Inflation / Purchasing Power Risk: Erosion of real returns on fixed nominal cash flows.
  • 5. Call / Prepayment Risk: Risk that the issuer calls back high-coupon bonds when rates fall.
  • 6. Liquidity Risk: Wide bid-ask spreads in thinly traded corporate bonds.

📐 Key Statutory Formulas, Thresholds & Valuation Equations
  • Cost-of-Carry Futures Pricing Model (No Income): Futures Price (F) = Spot Price (S) × e^(r·T) or F = S × (1 + r − y)^T (where r = risk-free rate, y = dividend/convenience yield)

  • Hedge Ratio (Minimum Variance Cross-Hedge): h* = ρ × (σ_S ÷ σ_F) (Number of Contracts N* = h* × (Size of Spot Position ÷ Size of 1 Futures Contract))

📐 Key Statutory Formulas, Thresholds & Valuation Equations
  • Call Option Intrinsic Value (at Spot S and Strike K): Max(S − K, 0) | Call Buyer Net Payoff (Profit): Max(S_T − K, 0) − Premium (c) | Call Breakeven: K + c

10.1 Key Multi-Leg Option Strategies (Straddle, Strangle, Bull/Bear Spreads)

  • Long Straddle (Volatility Play): Buying BOTH a Call and a Put at the same Strike Price (K) and same Expiry. Profits when the market makes a huge move in either direction (high volatility); maximum loss is the sum of the two premiums (c + p).
  • Long Strangle: Buying an Out-of-The-Money (OTM) Call and an OTM Put with different strike prices and the same expiry (cheaper upfront cost than a Straddle, but requires an even larger price move to break even).

  • Why Time Value of Money (TVM) Exists (4 Core Reasons Tested in Exams): A rupee received today is worth strictly more than a rupee received one year from now because of: (1) Opportunity Cost / Reinvestment Earning Potential (risk-free interest rate), (2) Inflation / Erosion of Purchasing Power, (3) Risk and Uncertainty of Future Cash Flows, and (4) Human Preference for Present Consumption over Future Consumption (Time Preference).
📐 Key Statutory Formulas, Thresholds & Valuation Equations
  • Future Value (Compounding m times per year): FV_n = PV × [1 + (r / m)]^(m · n) | Continuous Compounding: FV_n = PV × e^(r · n)
  • Present Value (Discounting): PV = FV_n ÷ (1 + r)^n (where 1/(1+r)^n is the Present Value Interest Factor / PVIF)
  • Effective Annual Interest Rate (EAR): EAR = [1 + (Nominal Rate r ÷ m)]^m − 1 (For m > 1, EAR is ALWAYS strictly greater than the Nominal Annual Rate r!)
  • Ordinary Annuity (Cash flow A at END of each period): PV = A × [(1 − (1 + r)^(−n)) ÷ r] | FV = A × [((1 + r)^n − 1) ÷ r]
  • Annuity Due (Cash flow A at BEGINNING of each period): PV_Due = PV_Ordinary × (1 + r) | FV_Due = FV_Ordinary × (1 + r)
  • Level Perpetuity (Infinite Cash Flow A): PV = A ÷ r | Growing Perpetuity (Gordon Constant Growth Model, r > g): PV = A_1 ÷ (r − g)
  • Doubling Time Shortcuts: Rule of 72 (Years to Double ≈ 72 ÷ Interest Rate %) | Rule of 69 (0.35 + 69/r) | Tripling Time: Rule of 114 (Years to Triple ≈ 114 ÷ r%)
  • Net Present Value (NPV): Σ [CF_t ÷ (1 + k)^t] − Initial Outlay (C_0) | Profitability Index (PI) = PV of Cash Inflows ÷ Initial Outlay (Accept if NPV > 0 and PI > 1)

20.1 Nominal Interest Rate vs. Real Interest Rate (Exact & Approximate Fisher Effect)

  • Exact Fisher Formula: (1 + Nominal Rate i) = (1 + Real Rate r) × (1 + Inflation Rate π) ⇒ r = (i − π) ÷ (1 + π).
  • Approximate Fisher Formula: Real Interest Rate (r) ≈ Nominal Interest Rate (i) − Inflation Rate (π). For example, if a bank FD pays 7.14% nominal interest and CPI inflation is 5.0%, the approximate real return is 2.14%, while the exact real return is (0.0714 − 0.05) ÷ 1.05 = 0.0214 ÷ 1.05 = 2.038%!
