🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (High-Low Method for Semi-Variable Overhead Segregation): Total factory overhead at 10,000 units of output is ₹1,40,000, and at 16,000 units is ₹1,88,000. Material Costing and Inventory Control (CAS-6)

5 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • CAS-6 Valuation of Material Receipts & Normal vs Abnormal Wastage: Under CAS-6, Material Cost includes purchase price, import duties, freight inward, transit insurance, and non-refundable taxes (excluding GST where Input Tax Credit is availed). 📐 Key Formulas, Statutory Thresholds & Core Identities
Economic Order Quantity (EOQ — Wilson's Formula): EOQ = √[ (2 × A × O) ÷ C ] where A = Annual Requirement in units, O = Ordering Cost per order, C = Carrying Cost per unit per annum
At EOQ Equilibrium: Total Annual Ordering Cost [ (A/EOQ) × O ] = Total Annual Carrying Cost [ (EOQ/2) × C ] = √(2 × A × O × C) / 2
Re-Order Level (ROL): Maximum Consumption × Maximum Re-Order (Lead) Period OR Minimum Level + (Average Consumption × Average Lead Time)
Minimum Stock Level (Safety Stock): Re-Order Level − (Normal/Average Consumption × Normal/Average Re-Order Period)
Maximum Stock Level: Re-Order Level + Re-Order Quantity (EOQ) − (Minimum Consumption × Minimum Re-Order Period)
Average Stock Level: (Minimum Level + Maximum Level) ÷ 2 OR Minimum Level + 1/2 (Re-Order Quantity / EOQ)
Danger Stock Level: Average (or Minimum) Consumption × Maximum Re-Order Period for Emergency Purchases
🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (EOQ & Stock Levels): Annual demand for raw material X = 36,000 units, Ordering cost = ₹250 per order, Cost per unit = ₹100, and Carrying cost = 20% p.a. (so C = 20% of ₹100 = ₹20/unit/year). Normal usage = 100 units/day, Min usage = 60 units/day, Max usage = 140 units/day, Lead time = 10 to 15 days (Average = 12.5 days). Step 1: EOQ = √[(2 × 36,000 × 250) ÷ 20] = √[1,80,00,000 ÷ 20] = √9,00,000 = 948.68 ≈ 949 units (if A=32,000, EOQ = √[(2×32,000×250)/20] = √8,00,000). Step 2: Re-Order Level (ROL) = Max Usage × Max Lead Time = 140 × 15 = 2,100 units. Step 3: Minimum Level = ROL − (Normal Usage × Normal Lead Time) = 2,100 − (100 × 12.5) = 850 units. Step 4: Maximum Level = ROL + EOQ − (Min Usage × Min Lead Time) = 2,100 + EOQ − (60 × 10)!
CH 03

Direct Labour Cost and Employee Cost Control (CAS-7)

2 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • CAS-7 Employee Cost Treatment of Overtime & Idle Time: Under CAS-7, Normal Idle Time (tea breaks, machine set-up, shift change) is treated as a part of Direct Cost (by inflating the hourly wage rate) or Production Overheads, whereas Abnormal Idle Time (power failure, strike, lock-out, flood, material stock-out) is strictly excluded from cost and debited directly to the Costing Profit & Loss Account.
  • Overtime Premium Accounting Rule (CAS-7): If overtime is worked at the specific request of a customer to expedite an urgent job, the overtime premium is charged directly to that Job; if worked regularly due to general capacity shortage, it is charged to Production Overheads.

🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (Halsey vs Rowan Earnings & Effective Hourly Rate): Standard time allowed for a job (S) = 20 hours; Actual time taken by worker (T) = 15 hours (so Time Saved S − T = 5 hours); Hourly wage rate (R) = ₹100/hr. (1) Under Halsey Plan (50%): Earnings = (15 × 100) + 0.50 × (5 × 100) = ₹1,500 + ₹250 = ₹1,750 (Effective hourly rate = ₹1,750 / 15 = ₹116.67/hr). (2) Under Rowan Plan: Earnings = (15 × 100) + (15 / 20) × (5 × 100) = ₹1,500 + ₹375 = ₹1,875 (Effective hourly rate = ₹1,875 / 15 = ₹125.00/hr). Notice that because Time Saved (5 hrs = 25% of S) is less than 50% of S, Rowan pays more than Halsey!

Overheads — Allocation, Apportionment and Absorption (CAS-3)

5 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • CAS-3 Production & Operation Overheads & CAS-2 Capacity Concepts: Under CAS-2 and CAS-3, fixed production overheads must be absorbed based on Normal Capacity (average production achieved over 3 normal years out of preceding 5 years) or Actual Capacity (if higher). Unabsorbed fixed overheads due to abnormal idle capacity are charged directly to Costing P&L.
  • Treatment of Under-Absorption and Over-Absorption of Overheads: If due to abnormal factors (defective planning, idle capacity) → transfer to Costing P&L; if due to genuine normal price/inflation changes across the factory → apply a Supplementary Overhead Rate across Cost of Sales, Closing Finished Goods, and Closing WIP!

🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (Reciprocal Simultaneous Equation Method): Production Depts P1 and P2 have primary overheads of ₹80,000 and ₹70,000. Service Dept S1 has primary overhead of ₹23,400 (gives 10% to S2) and Service Dept S2 has ₹30,000 (gives 20% to S1). Find total overheads of S1 (X) and S2 (Y) before final apportionment to P1 & P2. Set up equations: X = 23,400 + 0.20 Y and Y = 30,000 + 0.10 X. Substitute Y into X: X = 23,400 + 0.20(30,000 + 0.10 X) = 23,400 + 6,000 + 0.02 X → 0.98 X = 29,400 → X = ₹30,000. Then Y = 30,000 + 0.10(30,000) = ₹33,000!
CH 05

Activity Based Costing (ABC) and Activity Based Management (ABM)

0 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • Why Traditional Absorption Distorts Product Costs ('Peanut-Butter Costing'): Traditional volume-based costing (using a single plant-wide Machine Hour or Direct Labour Hour rate) over-costs high-volume simple products and under-costs low-volume complex products (which consume disproportionate batch set-ups, special inspections, and engineering change orders). ABC eliminates this cross-subsidization!
  • Time-Driven Activity Based Costing (TDABC — Kaplan & Anderson): Simplifies classic ABC by requiring only two parameters: (1) practical capacity cost rate per minute/hour of supplied resources, and (2) unit time equations for each activity variation.

🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (ABC vs Traditional Costing): A factory produces High-Volume Product A (10,000 units, 20,000 machine hrs, 5 set-ups) and Low-Volume Complex Product B (1,000 units, 5,000 machine hrs, 20 set-ups). Total Set-up Cost Pool = ₹2,50,000 (25 total set-ups). (1) Under Traditional Machine-Hour Absorption (₹2,50,000 / 25,000 MHs = ₹10/MH): Product B gets only 5,000 × ₹10 = ₹50,000 (₹50/unit), while Product A is over-charged ₹2,00,000! (2) Under Activity Based Costing (Set-up Driver Rate = ₹2,50,000 / 25 set-ups = ₹10,000 per set-up): Product B consumes 20 set-ups × ₹10,000 = ₹2,00,000 (₹200/unit), while Product A consumes only 5 set-ups × ₹10,000 = ₹50,000 (₹5/unit)!
CH 06

Unit Costing / Single Output Costing & Functional Cost Sheet

1 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • Modern ICMAI / ICAI Cost Sheet Revisions (Quality Control, R&D, Packing & Admin Overheads): Under the revised CAS-aligned Cost Sheet format tested in PFRDA and SEBI Grade A, Quality Control Cost (CAS-21), Research & Development Cost (CAS-18), Administrative Overheads relating to Production, and Primary Packing Cost (CAS-9) are all added BEFORE arriving at Cost of Production (COP)! Conversely, General Administrative Overheads and Secondary/Redistribution Packing are added AFTER Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) to arrive at Cost of Sales.
  • Items Strictly Excluded from Cost Accounts (Financial Charges & Appropriations): Income tax, dividend paid, cash discount, bad debts (financial), loss on sale of fixed assets, goodwill/preliminary expenses written off, and penalties/fines are NEVER included in a Cost Sheet.

2. Prime Cost = Direct Material Consumed + Direct Employee (Labour) Cost + Direct (Chargeable) Expenses
3. Gross Works / Factory Cost = Prime Cost + Factory / Production Overheads
4. Net Works / Factory Cost = Gross Works Cost + Opening Work-in-Progress (WIP) − Closing Work-in-Progress (WIP)

8. Profit & Sales Relationship: Sales = Cost of Sales + Profit | Markup on Cost = Margin on Sales ÷ (1 − Margin on Sales)
🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (Cost of Production & Closing Stock of Finished Goods Valuation): A manufacturer produces 10,000 units during the month: Prime Cost = ₹5,00,000, Works Cost = ₹7,20,000, Quality Control & Primary Packing = ₹30,000, General Admin Overheads = ₹40,000, Selling Overheads = ₹50,000. Step 2: Value of Closing Finished Goods (2,000 units) = 2,000 × ₹75 = ₹1,50,000. Step 3: COGS = ₹7,50,000 − ₹1,50,000 = ₹6,00,000. Step 4: Cost of Sales = COGS (₹6,00,000) + General Admin (₹40,000) + Selling (₹50,000) = ₹6,90,000!
CH 07

Job Costing and Batch Costing (Economic Batch Quantity — EBQ)

7 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • CA Raman Luthra Industry Classification for Job vs Batch Costing: Job Costing is used where every customer order is unique and customized (Printing Press, Interior Decoration, Automobile Repair Workshop, Custom Furniture, Machine Tools, Ship-building/Repair). Batch Costing is used where identical articles are manufactured in discrete lots/batches ahead of demand (Pharmaceuticals/Medicines, Readymade Garments, Footwear, Toys, Spare Parts, Biscuits/Confectionery, Watch Manufacturing).
  • Job Cost Sheet & Work-in-Progress Control Account: Each job is assigned a unique Job Number and tracked on a Job Cost Card; in the cost ledger, the Work-in-Progress (WIP) Control Account aggregates all active jobs.

0 PYQs
🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • CAS-19 Joint Costs Standard Rules: Under CAS-19, Joint Costs are the common costs incurred up to the Split-Off Point where two or more distinct products become separately identifiable. Realizable value of any By-Product (less post-split-off processing and selling costs) is deducted from the total Joint Cost before apportioning the remaining Joint Cost among the main Joint Products.

During the month, 1,000 units of Product Z were actually produced using 5,300 kg of raw material purchased at ₹19 per kg. Compute MCV, MPV, and MUV! Step 1: Standard Quantity for Actual Output (SQ) = 1,000 units × 5 kg = 5,000 kg; SP = ₹20; AQ = 5,300 kg; AP = ₹19. Step 2: Material Cost Variance (MCV) = (SQ × SP) − (AQ × AP) = (5,000 × 20) − (5,300 × 19) = ₹1,00,000 − ₹1,00,700 = ₹700 (Adverse). Step 3: Material Price Variance (MPV) = (SP − AP) × AQ = (20 − 19) × 5,300 = ₹5,300 (Favourable). Step 4: Material Usage Variance (MUV) = (SQ − AQ) × SP = (5,000 − 5,300) × 20 = ₹6,000 (Adverse). Verification: MPV (₹5,300 F) + MUV (₹6,000 A) = ₹700 (Adverse) = MCV!
