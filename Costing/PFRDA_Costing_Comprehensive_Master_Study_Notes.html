Economic Order Quantity (EOQ — Wilson's Formula): EOQ = √[ (2 × A × O) ÷ C ] where A = Annual Requirement in units, O = Ordering Cost per order, C = Carrying Cost per unit per annum
At EOQ Equilibrium: Total Annual Ordering Cost [ (A/EOQ) × O ] = Total Annual Carrying Cost [ (EOQ/2) × C ] = √(2 × A × O × C) / 2
Re-Order Level (ROL): Maximum Consumption × Maximum Re-Order (Lead) Period OR Minimum Level + (Average Consumption × Average Lead Time)
Minimum Stock Level (Safety Stock): Re-Order Level − (Normal/Average Consumption × Normal/Average Re-Order Period)
Maximum Stock Level: Re-Order Level + Re-Order Quantity (EOQ) − (Minimum Consumption × Minimum Re-Order Period)
Average Stock Level: (Minimum Level + Maximum Level) ÷ 2 OR Minimum Level + 1/2 (Re-Order Quantity / EOQ)
Danger Stock Level: Average (or Minimum) Consumption × Maximum Re-Order Period for Emergency Purchases

📘 2.1 Treatment of Material Losses — Waste, Scrap, Spoilage & Defectives (CA Raman Luthra + EduTap)

  • Waste: Portion of basic raw material lost in processing having NO recovery/realizable value (e.g., evaporation, smoke, shrinkage). Normal waste is absorbed by good units; abnormal waste is transferred to Costing P&L.
  • Scrap: Incidental residue from manufacturing having small realizable value without further processing (e.g., metal turnings, sawdust). Realizable value of normal scrap is credited to Factory Overheads or Cost of Production.
  • Spoilage: Goods damaged beyond economic rectification which must be disposed of as seconds/scrap without further processing.
  • Defectives: Units that fail quality inspection but CAN be rectified and brought up to standard by incurring additional material, labour, and overhead rectification cost.

🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • CAS-7 Employee Cost Treatment of Overtime & Idle Time: Under CAS-7, Normal Idle Time (tea breaks, machine set-up, shift change) is treated as a part of Direct Cost (by inflating the hourly wage rate) or Production Overheads, whereas Abnormal Idle Time (power failure, strike, lock-out, flood, material stock-out) is strictly excluded from cost and debited directly to the Costing Profit & Loss Account.

📘 3.1 Preventive vs Replacement Costs of Labour Turnover

  • Preventive Costs: Incurred by management to keep workers satisfied and prevent them from leaving the organization — e.g., medical benefits, pension/gratuity schemes, welfare amenities, better working conditions, and career training.
  • Replacement Costs: Incurred as a direct result of labour turnover when workers leave and new workers must be hired — e.g., recruitment/advertising cost, selection and induction training cost, lower productivity during learning period, and increased spoilage/tool breakage by new workers.

📘 4.1 CAS-2 Capacity Determination Definitions (Installed, Normal, Actual, Idle Capacity)

  • Installed Capacity: Maximum productive capacity according to manufacturers' specifications of machines/equipment.
  • Practical / Operating Capacity: Installed capacity minus inevitable internal interruptions (preventive maintenance, set-up time, holidays, shift changeovers).
  • Normal Capacity: Volume of production achieved or achievable on an average over a period under normal circumstances, taking into account loss of capacity resulting from planned maintenance (determined based on average of three normal years out of preceding five years).
  • Abnormal Idle Capacity: Difference between Normal Capacity and Actual Capacity utilization where actual capacity is lower due to abnormal external/internal factors.

🚀 2025–2026 Statutory, Regulatory & Current Affairs Updates
  • Why Traditional Absorption Distorts Product Costs ('Peanut-Butter Costing'): Traditional volume-based costing (using a single plant-wide Machine Hour or Direct Labour Hour rate) over-costs high-volume simple products and under-costs low-volume complex products (which consume disproportionate batch set-ups, special inspections, and engineering change orders). 📘 5.1 Structural vs Executional vs Transaction vs Duration Cost Drivers & Activity Based Management (ABM)

Physical Units / Average Unit Cost MethodApportions Joint Cost in proportion to physical weight, volume, or units produced at split-off.Used only when all joint products are measured in the same physical unit (e.g., tonnes) and have similar market prices.Fails when one joint product is a high-value gas and another is a heavy low-value solid residue.
2. Sales Value at Split-Off Point MethodApportions Joint Cost in proportion to the market sales value of each product at the exact split-off point (Output Units × Market Price at Split-Off).Best method when all joint products have a ready, quoted market price right at the split-off point.Generates an identical gross profit percentage for all joint products if sold at split-off.
3. Net Realizable Value (NRV) MethodApportions Joint Cost in proportion to (Final Sales Value − Post-Split-Off Further Processing Cost − Selling Expenses).Mandatory when joint products do NOT have a market price at split-off and must be processed further before sale.Most widely tested method in PFRDA and SEBI Grade A numericals!

📘 13.1 Four Types of Standards — Ideal, Normal, Basic & Current Standards

  • Ideal (Perfection) Standard: Established assuming 100% perfect operating conditions with zero machine breakdown, zero wastage, and peak efficiency (rarely achieved; can demotivate workers).
  • Normal / Attainable (Expected) Standard: Established under efficient operating conditions while making practical allowances for normal spoilage, normal machine maintenance, and normal fatigue (most widely used for cost control and motivation!).
  • Basic (Bogey) Standard: Unchanged long-term base-year standard kept constant over several years to measure long-term trends (like a price index).
  • Current Standard: Established for use over a short current period reflecting prevailing current conditions.

📐 Key Formulas, Statutory Thresholds & Core Identities
Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE in TPM — Seiichi Nakajima): OEE (%) = Availability (%) × Performance Efficiency (%) × Quality Rate (%)
where: Availability = (Actual Operating Time ÷ Planned Production Time) | Performance = (Ideal Cycle Time × Total Count) ÷ Operating Time | Quality Rate = (Good Units ÷ Total Units Started) [World-Class OEE Benchmark = 85% = 90% Avail × 95% Perf × 99.9% Quality]
Takt Time (Pace of Customer Demand) = Available Net Production Time per Shift ÷ Customer Demand per Shift
Target Costing Formula: Target Cost = Target Selling Price (Market-determined) − Target Profit Margin
Throughput Accounting Ratio (TPAR — Goldratt's Theory of Constraints): Return per Factory Hour = (Sales − Totally Variable Material Cost) ÷ Time on Bottleneck Resource | TPAR = Return per Bottleneck Hour ÷ Cost per Bottleneck Hour
Six Sigma Quality Standard (Motorola — Bill Smith, 1986): 3.4 Defects Per Million Opportunities (DPMO), corresponding to 99.99966% defect-free yield (including 1.5σ long-term shift)
🧮 Worked Numerical / Practical Case Study: Worked Numerical (TPM Overall Equipment Effectiveness — OEE Calculation): A shift is 480 minutes long, with 30 minutes of planned meal/tea breaks (so Planned Production Time = 480 − 30 = 450 minutes). During the shift, the machine suffered 45 minutes of unplanned breakdown/set-up downtime (so Actual Operating Time = 450 − 45 = 405 minutes). Ideal cycle time is 0.5 minutes per unit, and the machine produced 729 total units during the shift, of which 14.58 units (2%) were defective (so Good Units = 714.42, Quality Rate = 98%). Find Availability, Performance, Quality Rate, and OEE! Step 1: Availability = 405 ÷ 450 = 90.0%. Step 2: Performance Efficiency = (729 units × 0.5 min) ÷ 405 min = 364.5 ÷ 405 = 90.0%. Step 3: Quality Rate = 98.0%. Step 4: OEE = 0.90 × 0.90 × 0.98 = 79.38%!

📘 15.1 Taiichi Ohno's Seven Wastes (7 Muda + 8th Waste: TIMWOODS) & 3Ms (Muda, Mura, Muri)

  • The 3Ms of Toyota Production System: (1) Muda = Non-value-adding Waste; (2) Mura = Unevenness / Irregularity in workflow; (3) Muri = Overburden / Strain on workers or machines.
  • The 7 Classic Wastes (TIMWOOD) + 8th Waste (TIMWOODS):
    1. T — Transportation: Unnecessary movement of materials/products between processes.
    2. I — Inventory: Excess raw material, WIP, or finished goods hiding production problems.
    3. M — Motion: Unnecessary physical movement of workers (reaching, walking, bending).
    4. W — Waiting: Idle time waiting for materials, tools, inspection, or predecessor steps.
    5. O — Overproduction: Producing more, sooner, or faster than required by the next process (considered the worst of all wastes because it triggers all other wastes!).
    6. O — Over-processing: Doing more work or adding features/precision beyond what the customer values.
    7. D — Defects: Scrap, rework, and warranty repairs.
    8. S — Skills (8th Waste): Under-utilizing human talent, creativity, and shop-floor worker ideas.
